# Newsletter Brand Guideline — "Simple"

This guide translates the "Simple" brand system (as shown in the Brand Guidelines 2035 brochure) into rules for building a newsletter. Follow it so any newsletter issue looks consistent with the parent brand.

## 1. Brand Personality
- Bold, confident, modern, corporate-editorial.
- High contrast between dark/black sections and bright red accent sections.
- Architectural/urban photography (skylines, steel structures) reinforces a "built to last / structured" feel.
- Big, oversized display type used as a graphic element, not just text.

## 2. Color Palette

| Role | Color | Approx. Hex | Usage |
|---|---|---|---|
| Primary Black | Near-black charcoal | `#1A1A1A` | Cover backgrounds, headers, body text, footer bars |
| Primary Red | Signal red | `#C8202F` | Accent blocks, CTA buttons, links, section dividers, logo mark |
| Secondary Dark Red | Deep red | `#7A1620` | Gradient/overlay tint on photography, secondary accents |
| Neutral Gray | Mid gray | `#4D4D4D` | Secondary text, chart bars, muted UI elements |
| Off-White / Paper | Light neutral | `#F4F3F1` | Page/email background, content cards |
| Pure White | White | `#FFFFFF` | Text on dark/red backgrounds, logo reverse |

**Usage ratio guideline:** ~60% off-white/white background, ~25% black, ~15% red as accent. Red is never used as a large body-text color — only for headlines, tags, buttons, dividers, and the logo mark.

## 3. Typography

- **Display / Headline font:** A bold, tight, geometric grotesk (brand reference: *Neue Haas Grotesk Display Pro*, Bold/Black weight). Used oversized for section titles ("Brand Guidelines", "Table of Content", "Visual Identity").
- **Body font:** The same grotesk family in Regular/Medium weight for paragraph text, keeps a clean editorial feel.
- **Hierarchy:**
  - H1 / Issue title: 40–60px, Bold/Black, tight letter-spacing, often two-line stacked.
  - H2 / Section header: 20–28px, Bold, sometimes with the last word colored red.
  - Eyebrow / Label (e.g. "Defining Our Brand", "Welcome."): 10–12px, uppercase or small caps, gray or red, wide letter-spacing.
  - Body copy: 11–13px, Regular, gray-black (#333), generous line-height (~1.5).
  - Numbers/page markers (e.g. "01"): oversized, bold, used decoratively.

## 4. Layout & Grid
- Newsletter is structured in clear **modular blocks**, similar to a magazine spread: image block + text block side by side, or full-width color band as a divider.
- Generous white/negative space around headlines.
- Content organized into clearly labeled sections (mirroring the brochure's chapters): Welcome/Intro, Feature Story, Visual/Photo Highlight, Data/Stats block, Do's & Don'ts style tips block, Footer/Contact.
- Section dividers use a solid red or black full-bleed band with a short label.
- Photography is desaturated or duotoned (black/red overlay) to match brand rather than full color.

## 5. Imagery Style
- Black-and-white or monochrome-red-toned architectural, urban, or industrial photography (skyscrapers, steel/metal textures, city skylines).
- Portraits (e.g. team/leadership spotlights) are natural, well-lit, desaturated to match the palette.
- Avoid busy multi-color stock photography — keep it high-contrast and moody.

## 6. Newsletter-Specific Components
- **Header bar:** Logo top-left or top-right, issue date/number top-right, on black background.
- **Hero section:** Big bold headline over a duotone red/black image.
- **CTA buttons:** Solid red background, white bold text, sharp/slightly rounded corners.
- **Pull quotes:** Large red or black text, minimal quotation styling, no heavy decoration.
- **Dividers:** Thin red rule or full red color block between sections.
- **Footer:** Black background, white/gray text, logo reversed to white, contact info in small caps.

## 7. Tone of Voice
- Confident, concise, declarative sentence fragments for headlines ("Who We Are?", "Visual Identity").
- Body copy is straightforward and informational, not overly casual.

## 8. Do's and Don'ts (from brand system, applied to newsletter)
**Do**
- Keep sufficient clear space around the logo and headlines.
- Use red purposefully as an accent, not a background for large text blocks.
- Keep photography high-contrast/duotoned.

**Don't**
- Don't stretch, skew, or recolor the logo outside approved variants.
- Don't place red text on a red or busy background (low contrast).
- Don't mix in unrelated fonts or full-color photography.
